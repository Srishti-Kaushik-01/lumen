How do SANA diffusion models work?

How it works

SANA models are linear Diffusion Transformers built for fast, resource-efficient text-to-image generation. [1] They start from random noise and remove it step by step, using a simplified attention mechanism so that high-resolution images can be produced on modest hardware. [1] Later versions distil this process so that an image can be generated in as few as one to four steps. [2]

[1] SANA: Efficient High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Linear Diffusion TransformersEnze Xie et al. · 2025 · OpenAlex
[2] SANA-Sprint: One-Step Diffusion with Continuous-Time Consistency DistillationJunsong Chen et al. · 2025 · OpenAlex

Everything a research session needs

Lumen keeps the trail from question to source intact, so every answer can be checked and cited.

Context-aware follow-ups

Ask “What are its limitations?” and Lumen links it to the topic you were researching, and shows which topic it used.

Semantic ranking

A pretrained MiniLM transformer and FAISS rank papers by meaning, not just matching keywords.

Numbered citations

Every cited statement links to its paper with authors, year, and a DOI or arXiv link. Citations that do not match the paper are removed.

Written answers

An open-weight language model, hosted on Groq with Google Gemini as a backup, turns the retrieved papers into a clear explanation suited to your question: a definition, a comparison, or how something works.

Private history

Conversations are saved to your account. Search, rename, reopen, or delete them at any time.

Google sign-in

Sign in with your Google account or an email and password. Your data stays private to you.

How it works

A language model writes a clear answer from the ranked papers, cites each claim, and quotes the key evidence beneath it.

Questions

Where do the answers come from?

From abstracts of papers returned by OpenAlex and from a small curated collection. A language model writes the answer from those texts and cites each statement; each citation is checked against the paper, and quoted evidence is shown so you can check it yourself.

What happens if no paper answers my question?

Lumen does not answer from general knowledge. It says that it found too little relevant evidence and suggests rephrasing the question.

Is my research private?

Conversations are stored against your account only, and you can delete them or your whole account at any time. A short keyword query is sent to OpenAlex. To write the answer, your question, the two previous exchanges, and the paper summaries are sent to the language model service (Groq, or Google Gemini as a backup); your name, email address and password are never sent.

Does it need a GPU or a paid API?

No GPU is needed. Papers are ranked by a compact MiniLM transformer that runs on an ordinary CPU. Answers are written by a hosted language model through the free tiers of Groq and Google Gemini, which need an API key on the server. Without a key, Lumen still answers with quoted evidence.
